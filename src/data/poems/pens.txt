I like to chew on pens
because it proves them fallible.
In the store they look so damn
perfect. Shiny, identical
monuments to modern efficiency.
But not once they’re mine, oh no.
As soon as they’re away
from the safety of their clear plastic
and waxed cardboard,
feeling quite full of themselves,
thinking, maybe, they will be
the signers of important documents,
they find my teeth closing on them,
displacing soft plastic,
opening tiny black holes in paint.
And just to crush any will
that might remain in them,
I inform them that their now divotted
bodies will find, not the firm grip
of a CEO or congressmen, but instead
the incessant taping and twirling of a poet,
then smile cruelly as they sink into
the oblivion of despair.
