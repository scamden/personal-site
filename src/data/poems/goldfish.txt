I was still drunk when I woke and wobbled
into the grey diffusion of daylight
squeezing through the fog-glass bathroom windows.
(You’re not supposed to drink on Lexipro.)
Dirty footprints fish-netted the cream tile,
tracks from the night’s chases, the bird tracks
of high heels and after them the heavier
rubber tread where the bird-dogs had the scent.
Dirt became slick mud beneath bare feet
when I stepped shivering from the shower.
it squished between my toes and clung caked
all day despite all efforts to remove
it.  Black Moor floats belly up in his bowl
but isn’t dead; he struggles if you poke him.
